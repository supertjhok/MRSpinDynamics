# Envelope scope — source check 2026-09-05

Carrier examples below bound a review discussion; they are not a frozen aperture.
Dimensions can be internal, nominal or postal eligibility limits. Loaded outer
thickness, seams and bulging must be measured before geometric acceptance.

| Reference | Published dimensions | Evidence and interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| USPS large-envelope/flat maximum | 15 x 12 x 0.75 inches = 381 x 304.8 x 19.05 mm | Postal size limit, not a typical-envelope distribution |
| UPS Express Envelope, shipment guide | 12.5 x 9.5 inches = 317.5 x 241.3 mm | Nominal dimensions; filled outer thickness unspecified |
| FedEx envelope without pouch, Canada supplies page | 12.5 x 9.5 inches inside | Internal dimensions; not a mechanical outer-envelope specification |
| FedEx legal-size reusable envelope, same source | 15.5 x 9.5 inches inside | Illustrates why USPS maximum length does not cover every carrier envelope |

Sources:
- https://pe.usps.com/businessmail101?ViewName=Flats
- https://www.ups.com/media/en/prepare_shipment_guide_en.pdf
- https://www.fedex.com/en-ca/resources-tools/pack.html

Use the relevant regional carrier products when freezing scope; the FedEx source
is Canadian and does not by itself freeze a US product set. Under the provisional
5 mm-per-side interpretation, an approved loaded outer dimension D needs D +
10 mm clear span on that axis. This is a geometric allowance, not coil/shield
wall thickness. Leave max_outer_dimensions unresolved until the loaded product
set and clearance convention are confirmed.
